Food Security Optimization: Rice Yield vs. Nitrogen

SFM Field Application: Analyzing marginal returns in Malaysian Padi cultivation.

The Bioscience Problem

In Malaysia, optimizing Nitrogen (N) application is critical for food security. Under-fertilization leads to stunted growth, while over-fertilization results in lodging (falling over) and environmental nitrogen leaching into our waterways.

As a Food Security student, you use calculus to find the peak efficiency. The derivative f'(x) represents the Marginal Yield—the extra weight of rice gained for every additional kilogram of nitrogen applied.

The Mathematical Model

Yield Function f(x):

f(x) = -0.00015x² + 0.045x + 3.5

where x = Nitrogen (kg/ha), f(x) = Yield (Tons/ha)

First Derivative f'(x):

f'(x) = -0.0003x + 0.045

Represents: Rate of yield change per kg of N.
